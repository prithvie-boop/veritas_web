Sourcing home textiles from India

Bedding, towels and table linen: where to look, how to specify fabric and what international buyers must check.

Map the process to the cluster

Home textiles cover woven bedding, bath towels, table linen, curtains, cushions and printed fabric. A strong printing house may not make a good terry-towel supplier; a stitching unit may source fabric and dyeing elsewhere. Karur, Tamil Nadu is an established home-textile lead for table and kitchen linen, while Jaipur/Sanganer, Rajasthan is associated with printed and crafted textiles. Other production centers should be chosen by yarn, weave, dyeing, finishing and scale, then verified factory by factory.

Map the complete chain: spinning or yarn sourcing, weaving/knitting, dyeing, printing, finishing, cutting, sewing and packing. Ask which operations are in-house, what is subcontracted, and how each lot stays traceable.

Specifications that prevent disputes

For sheets, state fibre composition, yarn count, weave, finished dimensions, fabric mass and shrinkage tolerance after the specified wash. For towels, add pile construction, GSM, absorbency and loop-pull checks. For table linen and curtains, define drape, opacity, hem, seam strength and care method. All categories need color reference, shade variation limits, colorfastness, pilling where relevant, seam quality, needle controls and package presentation.

Approve a wash-tested sample, not only a fresh showroom piece. Keep a golden sample and test production bulk after finishing. Identify which lab reports apply to the exact fabric and colorway, especially for dyes, prints or coated finishes.

Labeling, claims and price

EU textile products generally require compliant fibre-composition labeling under Regulation 1007/2011; language and product-specific exceptions should be checked with the importer. Product-safety and chemical restrictions also depend on market and use. Verify “organic,” “recycled,” “easy care” or performance claims with chain-of-custody and test evidence rather than supplier copy. US and other destination rules differ by product and channel.

Quote at a defined size and weight with an agreed tolerances sheet. Include lab testing, packaging, barcode/label work, minimum dye lot, inspection and expected seconds rate in landed-cost comparisons.

2026–27 outlook and working with Veritas

Our reading: traceable inputs and substantiated care/sustainability claims will carry more weight in buyer decisions; this is an inference, not a projection of demand. The practical advantage goes to suppliers who can reproduce handfeel and shade after the first sample.
