Sourcing brass and metalware from India

Moradabad’s metal craft, alloy and finish choices, corrosion tests, and how to brief a metalware supplier.

Updated 4 November 2026 · 4 min read · By Veritas Consulting

Start with material, not appearance

Moradabad, Uttar Pradesh is the most recognizable starting point for Indian brass and art metalware, with casting, fabrication, engraving and finishing capabilities across the cluster. The same warm finish may conceal solid brass, brass-plated steel, aluminum or another base metal. Those products age, weigh, price and corrode differently. Write the base metal and alloy into the bill of materials, not just “antique brass finish.”

Typical programs include candleholders, bowls, hardware, tabletop accents, mirrors, planters and mixed-material décor. Assess whether a workshop can perform the specific casting, spinning, welding, machining or plating work in-house, and which stages are subcontracted.

Finish and functional tests

Specify alloy, wall thickness, weight range, weld appearance, thread engagement, edge safety, surface roughness, finish reference and allowable patina. For plated items, define the coating system and thickness and agree an appropriate adhesion and corrosion test. For lacquered items, assess scratch, humidity and cleaning resistance against the intended care instructions. A handmade sample may have intentional variation; a retail shipment still needs a measurable acceptance standard.

Check electroplating wastewater and chemical handling where relevant, as well as packaging that prevents metal-on-metal abrasion. Mixed materials need compatibility checks at joints and under temperature/humidity changes.

Market access and quoting

Do not treat a decorative bowl as food-contact approved. If it is for serving, the finished, accessible materials must be assessed for the destination market; the EU food-contact framework is one starting point. For consumer décor in the EU, review applicable General Product Safety Regulation duties with the importer. Verify tariff classification and any metal-content or labeling claims before production.

Compare quotes using the same metal weight, finish process, packaging and rejection standard. Ask how metal prices are fixed, whether the quote expires, what tooling is owned by whom and how finish consistency is checked lot to lot.

2026–27 view and the Veritas approach

EPCH reports 2025–26 artmetalwares exports close to the previous year's level; this is a handicraft category rather than all fabricated metal products. Our reading: documented base-metal composition and repeatable finishing will matter more for international retail programs as buyers tighten claim and safety reviews.
