Sourcing ceramics and pottery from India

Khurja and the product brief

Khurja in Bulandshahr district, Uttar Pradesh is an official ODOP pottery cluster. It is a useful search point for tableware, planters, tiles and decorative ceramics, but capabilities differ by clay body, kiln, glaze and firing regime. A decorative planter does not automatically qualify as a mug; begin with intended use, temperature exposure, care claims and the destination market.

Choose earthenware, stoneware or porcelain by the intended performance and available factory process rather than by a style label. Ask for actual firing range, absorption data and glaze formulation control. Small artisan batches can be desirable for character; larger retail orders need a method for consistent dimensions and color.

The test plan

Specify capacity, weight, dimensions, rim and foot profile, glaze coverage, decal position, surface finish and permitted speckle or pinhole range. Check wobble, crazing, handle strength, chipped edges, water absorption and thermal shock as relevant. For tableware, test the final decorated article for lead/cadmium migration and other applicable requirements. Validate dishwasher, microwave and oven claims individually; none should be inferred from the word “stoneware.”

Ceramics fail in transit at contact points. Define individual wraps, dividers, carton drop tests, stacking and pallet treatment. Review breakage after a trial shipment as part of the true cost.

Regulatory and commercial decisions

EU food-contact materials are governed by the framework Regulation (EC) 1935/2004 and GMP, with ceramic-specific and national requirements to check. US buyers should review FDA guidance and import controls on lead in foodwares. Requirements may change; have a qualified lab and importer determine the test regime for the exact item. For décor, avoid food-use imagery or claims unless validated.

Ask for batch traceability, kiln capacity, sample-to-production color control, MOQ per glaze, mold ownership and an agreed defect classification. Compare an ex-factory price with the fully packed, tested, landed unit cost.

2026–27 outlook and how Veritas helps

EPCH's combined stone and pottery handicraft category increased in 2025–26 versus 2024–25; it is not a ceramics-only growth forecast. Our reading: buyers of everyday tableware will prioritize migration-test evidence and fewer breakages while decorative buyers may accept more handmade variation if it is photographed and specified.
