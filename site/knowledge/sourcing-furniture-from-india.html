Sourcing furniture from India: the buyer’s guide

Wood species, construction, factory capability, packaging and the 2026 compliance questions that shape a reliable furniture program.

Choose the right production route

India can support carved solid-wood accent furniture, case goods, mixed-material tables, storage and upholstered pieces, but these are different supply chains. A carved console and a flat-pack cabinet should not be quoted against the same factory checklist. Start with a technical drawing, dimensions, material bill, target price, quantity, destination and intended retail channel; then match the process to a supplier.

Jodhpur, Rajasthan is a useful starting point for woodcraft and mixed-material furniture; EPCH identifies a trade facilitation facility there. Saharanpur, Uttar Pradesh is associated with carved wood products and has an EPCH technology centre with seasoning support. Jaipur can be useful for woodcraft development and testing. These are sourcing leads, not a claim that every workshop has export-scale capacity or the same finish quality.

Lock the specification before sampling

Ask whether the quote is for solid wood, veneer, plywood, MDF or a hybrid. Record the scientific/common species name, origin evidence, panel grade, veneer thickness, hardware, adhesive, finish chemistry and intended use. For wood, agree a moisture-content range appropriate to the destination climate and product construction, and test it on incoming stock and finished goods. Check seasoning, movement allowance, concealed joinery, drawer operation and wobble under load. Upholstered lines need a separate fabric, foam, frame and fire-safety specification.

Approve a sealed reference sample with a color/finish range, dimensions and tolerance table. Inspection should include structural/load tests suited to the item, sharp edges, stability, fastener torque, surface resistance, carton drop/stack tests and container loading. Record the acceptable quality limit and whether a failed test blocks shipment.

Landed cost and logistics

Compare ex-factory and FOB offers on the same bill of materials. A lower unit price can disappear when assembled volume, protective packaging, breakage, duties and inland freight are included. Ask for carton dimensions, net/gross weights, loading plan, spare parts, replacement policy, MOQ by finish, sampling charges and a realistic production calendar. For knock-down furniture, test assembly instructions and hardware completeness with someone who did not design it.

US and EU checks for 2026

For US imports, the Lacey Act declaration may apply to covered wood products and the precise tariff classification matters. Composite-wood panels and certain finished products can trigger EPA TSCA Title VI formaldehyde requirements, including certification, labeling and records. EPA finalized a quality-control test-method update in October 2026; confirm the implementation details for your products. For EU sales, check whether the exact commodity and CN code fall within the EU Deforestation Regulation. Its main application date is 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators, with later timing for most micro and small operators; documentation should be designed early. Consumer furniture also needs a product safety review under applicable EU rules. Have your importer or specialist confirm the actual obligations for the SKU and market.
