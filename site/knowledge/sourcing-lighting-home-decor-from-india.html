India sourcing guide / Lighting & décor

Sourcing lighting and home décor from India

Build a cross-material collection while controlling electrical safety, finish consistency, assembly and export packaging.

Updated 4 November 2026 · 4 min read · By Veritas Consulting

A collection is often multi-cluster

Decorative lamps, mirrors, lanterns, candleholders, wall accents and seasonal décor often combine metal, glass, wood, textiles and stone. Moradabad is a lead for metal components, Firozabad for glass, and Saharanpur/Jodhpur for woodcraft. Those regional associations help identify makers, but a finished lamp may require a separate electrical assembly and testing partner. Assign one accountable supplier to the complete product and all subcontracted parts.

Separate decorative and powered products

For décor, specify material, size, finish, edge safety, hanging hardware, load rating and the intended installation surface. For a powered lamp, additionally define mains voltage, plug and cord by destination, lampholder, earth/insulation design, heat management, wiring strain relief and replaceability. Have a qualified test lab determine applicable electrical standards and market approvals for the finished model; a compliant component alone does not establish compliance for the assembled lamp.

Build a master sample for every finish and check visual consistency across mixed materials. Test tip stability for floor and table lamps, assembly instructions, replacement parts and transit protection for fragile shades or glass.

Claims, cost and supplier control

Do not market a decorative lantern as electrical lighting without a separate engineering and compliance route. EU consumer products require an appropriate safety review, and powered products may face additional electrical, EMC and other rules depending on the design. Destination-specific requirements and certification marks should be confirmed with the importer and test lab before production.

Quote the complete assembly, not a metal base alone: include glass, electrical kit, finishing, testing, instructions, carton design and spare parts. Agree who owns drawings/tooling, who signs off subcontractor changes, and how the final packed product is inspected.

2026–27 outlook and the Veritas role

Our reading: the strongest sourcing route is likely to be a coordinated cluster network with one documented bill of materials and one accountable final-assembly quality plan. This is a process prediction, not a claim about market growth.

Veritas can bring the metal, glass and wood components into one sourcing plan, coordinate the responsible final-assembly supplier and follow the finished product through testing, sample approval and a packed pilot.
