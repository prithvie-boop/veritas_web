Why US buyers are adding India to their sourcing mix

India is one of the few countries that can supply cotton textiles, handmade rugs, solid-wood furniture, metalware, glass and ceramics at retail scale from a single sourcing base.

Diversification

A real alternative, not a backup

India has depth in categories where US buyers have relied on one country: cotton bedding and bath, cushions and throws, tufted and woven rugs, accent furniture and decorative metal.

Capability

Handmade at retail volume

Hand-knotting, block printing, wood carving and metal casting sit alongside mechanised mills, so one programme can combine craft detail with consistent bulk production.

Raw materials

Cotton, wool, wood and metal at source

India grows its own cotton and has established supply for wool, jute, mango and acacia wood, brass and glass, which shortens the supply chain behind each product.

Duties & trade status

US import duties on Indian home goods today

Tariffs have moved several times since 2025, so US buyers need current numbers when they cost a programme. This is where things stand.

No trade deal yet

As of November 2026 the US and India have not signed a trade agreement. The 18% rate announced in the February 2026 framework was not applied after the US Supreme Court ruled that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs.

10% on top of the normal rate

Most Indian goods currently pay a 10% Section 301 duty, in place since 24 July 2026, in addition to the normal duty for the product's HTS code. The base rate differs widely between, say, cotton bedding and wooden furniture.

Product-specific tariffs

Separate Section 232 tariffs apply to certain metal and wood products and can change the landed cost of metalware and some furniture. We cost each item at HTS-code level before you commit.

Last reviewed November 2026. Duty rates and trade rules change; confirm the rate for your exact tariff code with your customs broker before costing an order.

US compliance, planned before production starts

Most compliance failures are specification failures. We write the applicable US requirements into the product brief and test plan at the sampling stage, and coordinate testing with the accredited lab you nominate.

Textiles & rugs

Labelling, flammability and cotton traceability

What US buyers typically need for soft home goods.

  • FTC fibre content, care and country-of-origin labelling
  • Carpet and rug flammability (16 CFR 1630 / 1631)
  • Cotton origin documentation to support UFLPA due diligence
  • California Proposition 65 chemical review
Furniture

Wood, finishes and stability

Requirements that shape design and material choices early.

  • TSCA Title VI formaldehyde limits for MDF, plywood and particleboard
  • Lacey Act plant declaration for wood species and origin
  • STURDY tip-over standard for clothing storage units (16 CFR 1261)
  • Lead limits in paints and surface coatings
Children's products

CPSIA testing and certification

Any item designed for children 12 and under carries extra obligations.

  • Third-party testing at a CPSC-accepted lab
  • Children's Product Certificate (CPC)
  • Permanent tracking labels
  • Lead and phthalate limits
Tabletop & décor

Food contact and retailer manuals

Ceramics, glass and metal items that touch food need their own checks.

  • FDA lead and cadmium limits for ceramic and glass foodware
  • Proposition 65 warnings where required
  • Retailer packaging, drop-test and labelling manuals
  • Social compliance audits your retailer accepts
Where it is made

India's home-product clusters, matched to your range.

Indian home goods are made in specialised regional clusters, and capability varies sharply between them. Choosing the right cluster and the right factory within it decides cost, quality and lead time.

Home textiles & rugs

Panipat for throws, cushions, bath mats and rugs; Karur for kitchen and table linen; Bhadohi and Mirzapur for hand-knotted and hand-tufted carpets; Jaipur for block prints and flatweaves. Read our guides to home textiles and rugs and carpets.

Furniture

Décor, lighting & tabletop

Lead times and shipping from India to the US

Plan the calendar backwards from your in-store date. These are indicative ranges; we confirm them factory by factory.

Development

Sampling: 3 to 6 weeks

Depends on category and how much is new. Hand-knotted rugs and new furniture designs take longest; repeat textile constructions are fastest.

Production

Bulk: 8 to 14 weeks

From approved sample and confirmed order. Handmade categories and peak season before Diwali need extra margin.

Transit

Sea freight: about 4 to 7 weeks

From Mundra or Nhava Sheva to US East Coast ports is usually faster than to the West Coast. Routing changes can add time, so we track bookings early.

FAQ

Sourcing from India for the US: common questions

Short answers to the questions US buyers ask us most often.

What is the US import duty on home goods from India?

As of November 2026, most Indian goods pay the normal duty for their HTS code plus a 10% Section 301 duty that has applied since 24 July 2026. Some metal and wood products also fall under Section 232 tariffs. Rates change, so confirm your exact code with a customs broker.

Is India a realistic alternative to China for home products?

For cotton home textiles, rugs, solid-wood furniture, metalware, glass and ceramics, yes. India is weaker in categories that depend on large-scale plastics, electronics or highly automated assembly. Many US importers run India alongside existing suppliers rather than replacing them outright.

What minimum order quantities should we expect?

It varies by category and factory. Handmade products such as rugs and carved furniture often allow smaller runs, while mill-made textiles need larger quantities per colour. We match your volumes to factories of the right size instead of forcing a large mill to take a small order.

How does Veritas control quality for US orders?

Quality is planned before production with clear specifications and approved samples, then checked through in-line and final inspections before shipment approval. When something goes wrong we run root-cause analysis and corrective actions with the factory.

Can you work with our existing Indian suppliers?

Yes. We can manage follow-up, inspections and compliance for factories you already use, and introduce new suppliers only where there is a gap in capability, capacity or price.

Tell us what you want to source.

Share your category, target price, volumes and destination. We will come back with a view on the right clusters, realistic lead times and how we would run the programme.
