Duties & trade status

UK and EU import duty on Indian home goods

The UK and the EU are at different stages. Buyers in each market should plan on the rules that apply today.

United Kingdom: in force

The India-UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement entered into force on 15 July 2026 and gives duty-free access for about 99% of India's exports to the UK, including textiles, provided goods meet the rules of origin.

European Union: not yet in force

India and the EU concluded negotiations on 27 January 2026, and the European Commission sent the agreement to the Council for approval in September 2026. Until it is ratified and applied, current EU duty rates remain.

What changes when it applies

The agreement is set to remove EU duties of up to 12% on Indian textiles and to improve access for furniture and handicrafts. Origin documentation will decide who benefits, so we prepare for it now.

Last reviewed November 2026. Duty rates and trade rules change; confirm the rate for your exact tariff code with your customs broker before costing an order.

European compliance, planned before production starts

European rules reach deep into the supply chain. We write the applicable requirements into the product brief and test plan at sampling stage and collect the evidence your compliance team needs.

Wood & furniture

EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)

Wooden furniture is in scope from 30 December 2026 for importers of every size.

  • Wood traced to the plot of land where it was grown
  • Evidence the land was not deforested after 31 December 2020
  • Species and origin records for your due diligence statement
  • FSC-certified options where the range calls for them
Chemicals

REACH and restricted substances

Applies to dyes, finishes, coatings and metal components.

  • Azo dye and other REACH Annex XVII restrictions
  • Substances of very high concern (SVHC) declarations
  • OEKO-TEX Standard 100 for textiles where required
  • Your own restricted substances list, tested at an accredited lab
Product safety

GPSR, labelling and standards

General product safety rules apply to almost every consumer item.

  • EU General Product Safety Regulation documentation and traceability
  • Fibre composition labelling for textiles
  • UK fire-safety regulations for upholstered furniture
  • CE or UKCA marking for lighting; food-contact rules for tableware
Social & environmental

Audits and supply-chain due diligence

Retailers increasingly ask for evidence beyond the product itself.

  • amfori BSCI or Sedex SMETA social audits
  • Health, safety and wage compliance checks at the factory
  • GOTS or recycled-content certification for relevant ranges
  • Supplier mapping beyond the final assembly unit

Lead times and shipping from India to Europe

Plan the calendar backwards from your launch date. These are indicative ranges; we confirm them factory by factory.

Development

Sampling: 3 to 6 weeks

Depends on category and how much is new. Hand-knotted rugs and new furniture designs take longest; repeat textile constructions are fastest.

Production

Bulk: 8 to 14 weeks

From approved sample and confirmed order. Handmade categories and the weeks before Diwali need extra margin.

Transit

Sea freight: about 4 to 6 weeks

From Mundra or Nhava Sheva to main UK and North European ports, depending on routing. Mediterranean ports are usually quicker.

FAQ

Sourcing from India for Europe: common questions

Short answers to the questions UK and European buyers ask us most often.

Is the India-EU free trade agreement in force?

Not yet. Negotiations concluded on 27 January 2026 and the European Commission submitted the agreement to the Council in September 2026. It still needs approval and ratification on both sides, so current EU duty rates apply until then.

Do Indian home goods enter the UK duty-free?

The India-UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement entered into force on 15 July 2026 and gives duty-free access for about 99% of India's exports, provided the goods meet the agreement's rules of origin and carry the right origin documentation.

Does the EUDR apply to wooden furniture from India?

Yes. Wooden furniture is covered from 30 December 2026 for importers of every size. Importers need to trace the wood to the plot where it was grown and show the land was not deforested after 31 December 2020.

Which certifications can Indian factories provide?

Depending on the factory and category, common ones include OEKO-TEX, GOTS, FSC, amfori BSCI and Sedex SMETA. We verify that each certificate is current and that its scope covers the product you are buying.

Can you handle smaller or mixed orders for independent retailers?

Often, yes. Handmade categories usually allow smaller runs, and we plan consolidation so products from several factories can ship in one container.

Tell us what you want to source.

Share your category, target price, volumes and destination. We will come back with a view on the right clusters, realistic lead times and how we would run the programme.
